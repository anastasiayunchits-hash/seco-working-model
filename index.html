The working model

Every box we have, with everything proposed for it. This is the one we edit. The presentation copy is a separate page and does not change when this does.

What the whole thing is for, before any of the detail. Two steps, and a loop: measure her until the model can guess, then change one thing on purpose and see if it held.

01

The model as it stands

Two see-through boxes running along time. Inside each, the same four boxes as on the mechanism map, in the same square. Inside those, the named boxes. Inside those, the days.

The axis is minutes, not days. Ninety days is the same width it always was, but the zoom keeps going. Press Into this day and everything outside the day the slider is on is cut away, so the same nest of boxes is left holding one day. It runs six in the morning to six the next morning, and the marks under the model stop saying days and start saying the time. Press the button again for the run back.

The environment box is split by how fast a thing moves, because that decides what the model can do with it. Around you and conditions are the moment: they change within a day, so they can cut a baseline in two, her heart at home against her heart at work. The standing floor is the bottom tier, and it is two boxes: environmental safety, whether the ground is there at all, and meaning and agency, whether the life on top of it is worth having. Somebody can have one without the other. It holds for weeks or months and it cannot cut anything, because if she has been housed all year there is no unhoused half of her to compare against. What it does instead is move where her whole normal sits. What happens is dated and then over, and those are the things a baseline is used to judge, never the yardstick.

Work is a place and a thing she does, so it is in the model twice. The workplace is a place, beside home and outside. Working is a stretch of her doing it, over in the person box with her other behaviour. That split is what lets all four be true: at the workplace working, at the workplace on a break, at home working, at home not. Two of her five working days are worked from home, and on those the working pipe runs across the hours she is also at home. Nothing in the model requires a box to be alone.

People reach the model two ways. How much of the day had people in it is a daily tap, because a tap is a good instrument for a total and a bad one for an instant. Everything else is told at length and believed for months: who she lives with, who she could turn to, and whether those relationships are any good. Structure, availability, quality. Three questions that can disagree with each other, which is why they are kept apart: somebody can live with people, have nobody to call, and say the relationships are fine.

There was a third way and it is gone. Asking is anyone with you right now in the moment gave twelve answers across ninety days, which is thirty six minutes of labelled time out of a hundred and thirty thousand. A context cannot split a baseline on that, and the asking landed on her hardest afternoons, which is where it feels least like care. If the companion wants to know, it asks in conversation and the answer stays there.

Nothing senses anything on the standing floor. Every value in it is one she tells us, so each carries the date she answered and loses confidence as it ages rather than quietly changing to something she never said. They are the same fifteen things as needs in the person box, read as standing facts about a situation.

A dashed box is one nobody has agreed to yet. It is there so it can be argued with, not because it is settled.

Press one of the tags under the slider and the rest of the model steps back, so you see that one thing on its own and where it sits. Press it again to bring everything back. The tags are the things that come and go: environment, state and behaviour. What the sensors record is there every day, so it is not listed.

Hover a box and it says its name. Nothing else is written on the model, so nothing covers anything. The full list is written out underneath.

Scroll to move around the model without turning it, up and down or side to side. On a mouse with only one wheel, hold shift to go sideways. Hold ctrl or cmd and scroll to zoom in on whatever is under the pointer. Dragging still spins it, and Default puts it back.

03

From a device to a reading

Everything above assumes a number arrived and meant something. This is the path it travels to get there, and the two places where it can quietly stop meaning what it says.

The shape of it is one layer per question, so that swapping a sensor does not mean rebuilding the model on top of it.

1 · Device adapters

Every device speaks its own language. The adapter brings it in without losing anything: the raw signal, the real timestamps, the units, and which device it came from.

Nothing is thrown away here, and that is the whole rule. The band writes about twenty one megabytes a night, and every feature argued over in the sections above was recomputed from that raw file more than once. An adapter that kept only summaries would have made all of it impossible.

2a · Signal quality and measurement confidence

Can I trust this particular minute? Asked of every minute on its own: was the waveform clipped, did the accelerometer latch, was there movement, how many beats were clean.

This layer exists already and its doctrine is right: it refuses rather than discounts, so a minute either carries a reading or carries a written reason why not. What is wrong is what it refuses on. Measured on 23 October, minutes of ordinary arm movement read 7 to 16 mg and passed the 15 mg gate, while being four times worse than the still ones. The gate is in the right place and watching the wrong thing.

2b · Device validation and reference comparison

How good is this device, in general? Asked once per device, against a reference, across conditions. This is the layer the engine does not have at all.

It is what one session with a chest ECG produced: the wrist is about 1.2 bpm from the chest at rest and 20.9 ms out on variability, rising to 83.8 ms with arm movement. It is also what turns a confidence number from a feeling into a measurement, which the engine's own comment admits it is not yet: the ceilings are ordinal, not calibrated.

3 · Common physiological feature layer

One agreed definition of each quantity, so everything downstream reads the same vocabulary whatever produced it.

The trap is here. Chest variability and wrist variability are not the same quantity: 37 ms against 57 on the same minutes. If they share a field name, something downstream will average them, and no confidence score prevents that. They have to arrive as distinct quantities carrying where they came from, not as one row called HRV.

This is the feature layer drawn out. Three things the band carries, each described the same nine ways, so what reaches the model is twenty seven markers rather than one or two summary numbers.

The point of drawing it is what it does to RMSSD. Everything above treats the brake as the centre of the reading, and three days of measurement went into one cell of this grid. On the grid it is the heart's smoothness, sitting beside eight other descriptions of the same signal and eighteen descriptions of two others.

That matters for the problem in section 03. The wrist's smoothness is the cell we cannot yet trust, 20.9 ms out at rest. It is not the only cell. The level, the two activation shapes, the two deactivation shapes and the swing are all computed from the same beats and none of them has been checked against the chest. A reading built from several of them might survive one of them being poor, which is a different design from one that rests on the brake alone.

The two grey cells are honest absences. Nothing computes how the pulse height repeats or how steadily it repeats, because nobody has asked for them. They are drawn so that the question stays visible rather than being forgotten.

4 · Personal physiological model

Your middle and your spread, learned from your own minutes, so a reading can say how far out you are in your own terms rather than against a population.

Keyed on method, device and pipeline, because all three move the number. The method decides whether it is even the same quantity. The device decides the bias within that quantity. The pipeline decides it too: two detectors on identical bytes gave 20.9 ms and 9.1 ms. Baselines are merged only when a comparison has shown they agree, never by assumption.

5 · Psychological interpretation and app

Physiology joined to what she said and what was happening, shown with its uncertainty rather than as a verdict.

One thing belongs up here that is easy to lose: the rules about how a question may be asked. A prompt that announces why it arrived collects an answer about the detector rather than about the person. That is a measurement constraint, not interface styling, and if it lives only in the app it dies with the next rewrite.

C — Conventional physiological metrics

What established physiological quantities can we calculate?

HR, RMSSD, SDNN, respiratory rate, EDA tonic level, SCR frequency and other validated features.

Multimodal pattern-recognition model

Compare:

  • Raw signals alone.
  • Your 27 descriptors alone.
  • Conventional metrics alone.
  • Combinations of all three.

This allows you to investigate whether your descriptors actually add predictive information.

For example, does your activation-time feature improve prediction compared with a model that receives only raw HR data?

If it doesn't, perhaps the neural network can learn that information independently.

If it does, your feature engineering is adding something useful, especially when data are limited.

6. One major issue: raw data requires more training examples

This is where I would be careful.

Imagine you have 49 hours of physiological recordings but only three reliably labelled psychological episodes.

A neural network cannot automatically infer which signal patterns correspond to distress just because it has thousands of measurements.

It needs examples linking patterns to meaningful outcomes.

There are techniques such as self-supervised learning and contrastive learning that can help a model learn useful representations from large quantities of unlabelled data. But they don't eliminate the need for reliable labels when you eventually want to recognise psychological experiences.

You should also test whether the model generalises to new people, rather than simply recognising the individual patterns of the people in its training data.
